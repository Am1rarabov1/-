How Dusk was made

Dusk is part of the Atelier Collection — sister sites to Atelier built autonomously by Claude (Fable 5). Its premise: the page is one evening. You scroll from 17:42 to 23:58, and everything — the light of the page itself included — keeps time with you.

i.The palette is the protagonist

The whole design lives in seven CSS custom properties (background, foreground, accent, hairlines, ghost numerals). As each time-chapter approaches, GSAP scrubs those variables between phases with gsap.utils.interpolate: warm parchment at golden hour, a mauve-grey blue hour, a plum night, and a near-black midnight. The page literally gets darker as the evening gets later — the scroll bar is a sunset.

ii.A clock you scroll

A fixed rail on the right shows the time of evening, computed from scroll progress: 17:42 + (23:58 − 17:42) × progress, with a thin amber line filling as the night advances. The chapter markers in the nav are times, not names. It turns the scrollbar into narrative time — the cheapest "wow" per line of code on any of these six sites.

iii.Chapter choreography

  • Ghost numerals — each chapter's time (19:06, 21:30…) sits behind the content at ~26vw, drifting horizontally on scrub like a watermark in motion.
  • Un-clipping images — every photograph starts matted (inset(6% 8%)) and relaxes to full-bleed as it approaches center, while the image inside counter-parallaxes and settles from scale(1.12).
  • Alternating layouts — chapters flip left/right via direction: rtl on the grid, so the eye zigzags down the evening.
  • A starfield finale — 70 CSS-animated stars twinkle in over the last-light photograph, each with a random period and delay.

iv.The stack & the recipe

  • Static HTML, GSAP + ScrollTrigger + SplitText, Lenis. Cormorant Garamond and Jost, self-hosted.
  • Photography generated with Higgsfield to a time-of-day brief (same room, different hours); the golden-hour hero was animated with Kling 3.0 Turbo — drifting dust motes in the sun shafts.
  • To repeat: choose a dimension that maps naturally onto scroll (time, depth, altitude, seasons), express it in CSS variables, and scrub them. One good mapping beats twenty animations.
  • prefers-reduced-motion pins the site to a single readable phase with no scrubbing.
