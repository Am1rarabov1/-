How Atelier was made

Atelier is the third of three Smart Installers experiences designed and built autonomously by Claude (Fable 5) — and it is deliberately the softest. Where CURRENT is a WebGL night-drive and The Signal Standard is ink on newsprint, Atelier is dusk light and photography. Three universes, one company. Here is how this one works.

i.Photography-first design

The concept — "technology you feel, not see" — forbids the usual smart-home clichés: no glowing app screenshots, no floating dashboards. Instead the site is carried by a set of AI-generated photographs (Higgsfield's nano banana pro model) shot to one lighting brief: dusk, warm practicals waking up, bone/sage/espresso palette, film grain, nobody home. Because every image obeys the same brief, they read as one architectural shoot.

The hero goes further: the lead photograph was handed to Kling 3.0 Turbo (via Higgsfield) as a start frame and animated into a slow push-in where the lamps gradually brighten — a cinemagraph, not a stock loop. It ships as a muted, looping, playsinline video with the original still as its poster, and reduced-motion users get the still.

ii.Type as scent, not shout

DisplayItaliana — a hairline engraver's roman, letterspaced hard (0.18em) so ATELIER reads like a plaque, not a logo.
VoiceCormorant Garamond italic for asides and numerals — the handwriting in the margins.
BodyKarla light — plain enough to disappear.
PaletteBone #ede7dc · espresso #2a211b · sage #8a9b8e · candle amber #b98a4a. The photographs supply all the warmth.

iii.The two set pieces

  • Room chapters with a sticky lens. The Rooms section is a two-column grid: the left column is position: sticky for a full viewport height while four text chapters scroll past on the right. ScrollTrigger watches each chapter and crossfades the corresponding photograph in the sticky frame (opacity + a slow settle from scale(1.08)), with a Cormorant numeral marking the room. It reads like turning pages in a monograph.
  • The Dial. A real interaction, not a video of one: four lighting scenes (Entertain / Screening / Unwind / Goodnight) re-light the same photograph live using layered CSS — a gradient wash in mix-blend-mode: multiply plus brightness/sepia filters — while the caption rewrites itself. It demonstrates the product (scenes) with the medium (light) instead of explaining it.

iv.Motion at candle speed

Lenis runs the scroll with a low lerp (0.085) so the page glides. Every reveal is the same quiet move — 40px rise, one second, power3.out — because in a luxury register, variety of animation is noise. The only theatrical beat is the opening: the word ATELIER tightens its letterspacing out of a bone-colored veil, then the video fades up already moving. All of it collapses gracefully under prefers-reduced-motion.

v.The stack

MarkupHand-written static HTML — no framework, no build.
MotionGSAP 3 + ScrollTrigger + SplitText, Lenis. Self-hosted.
MediaHiggsfield image model for stills, Kling 3.0 Turbo for the hero cinemagraph; optimized to JPEG/H.264 in CI with ffmpeg.
HostingGitHub Pages via Actions; the folder deploys to Netlify unchanged: netlify deploy --prod --dir atelier.

vi.Repeat this yourself

  1. Write one lighting brief and generate every image with it. Consistency of light is what makes AI photography read as a real shoot.
  2. Animate only your lead image (image-to-video, "slow push-in, practicals brighten") — one living photograph beats ten stock clips.
  3. Pick a hairline display face and letterspace it; put your personality in an italic serif, and keep body text invisible.
  4. Build one honest interaction that demonstrates the product — a dial, a toggle, a slider — instead of describing it.
  5. Use one reveal animation everywhere. Restraint is the luxury.
  6. Critique your own screenshots at least three times before shipping. This site went through three passes.
