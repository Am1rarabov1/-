How this paper was typeset

The Signal Standard was written, art-directed and built autonomously by Claude (Fable 5). Where the first Smart Installers experience (CURRENT) is a dark WebGL film, this one is its deliberate opposite: a broadsheet. Same subject, different universe — that contrast is the point, and this page explains the machinery.

01One metaphor, obeyed everywhere

The concept: a trade journal from the golden age of print, reporting on low-voltage craft. Once the metaphor is chosen, every design decision has a test — "would a 1950s pressroom do this?" Sections become SEC. A/B/C, services become classified listings with dot leaders, stats become a spec sheet, the CTA becomes a cut-out coupon with a dashed border and a scissors glyph. Nothing on the page breaks character.

02Typography is the hero

DisplayFraunces, a variable font with four axes. The masthead runs it at font-variation-settings: 'opsz' 144, 'WONK' 1 — maximum optical size for razor-thin contrast, with the "wonky" alternates on for old-press personality.
TextArchivo for running copy — a grotesque with newspaper DNA.
DataIBM Plex Mono for folios, captions, tickers and fine print.
DetailReal drop caps (float: left), two-column justified lede with a column rule, double-rule borders (border: 4px double) and dot leaders built from border-bottom: 2px dotted.

The palette is three inks: paper #f6f1e5, ink #171512, and safety orange #ff5200 — the color of pull-tape and warning labels, which is exactly what a wiring journal would keep in the drawer.

03The print effects

  • Halftone photography. Every photo is AI-generated in high-contrast black and white, then screened in CSS: a repeating radial-gradient dot matrix layered over the image with mix-blend-mode: overlay. On hover the ink "re-saturates" — a sepia/hue-rotate filter reveals warmth, like a print developing.
  • Paper grain. A fixed SVG turbulence texture multiplied over the whole page at 6% opacity. Cheap, GPU-friendly, and it makes flat color feel like stock.
  • Velocity skew. The big serif blocks shear by up to 4° based on scroll velocity (GSAP quickTo + ScrollTrigger's getVelocity()), so the type feels like it is being dragged across a press bed.
  • The self-drawing schematic. A hand-coded SVG of a house is revealed with the classic stroke-dasharray / stroke-dashoffset trick, scrubbed by scroll: the house draws first, then the orange low-voltage runs, then the labeled nodes pop in. Two orange pulses then travel the runs forever via getPointAtLength().

04Motion with restraint

Print doesn't fade — it arrives. Masthead words rise out of masked containers like plates being set; field notes land with a half-degree rotation like photographs laid on a desk; the coupon wobbles in with a back.out ease. Lenis provides the smooth scroll, bridged into GSAP's ticker. Everything respects prefers-reduced-motion.

05The stack (deliberately boring)

MarkupHand-written HTML. No framework, no build step.
MotionGSAP 3 + ScrollTrigger + SplitText, Lenis. All self-hosted.
ImageryHiggsfield nano banana pro, prompted for "1950s trade journal photography, heavy film grain" so the halftone treatment lands on honest source material.
HostingStatic folder on GitHub Pages; deploys unchanged to Netlify with netlify deploy --prod --dir signal.

06Repeat this yourself

  1. Pick a physical medium your subject would live in (a newspaper, a passport, a field manual, a receipt) and commit totally.
  2. Choose one variable font with personality and learn its axes — half this site's character is 'opsz' 144.
  3. Steal print furniture: rules, folios, dot leaders, drop caps, captions that start with FIG.
  4. Convert imagery to your medium (halftone screens here) so photos obey the same physics as the type.
  5. Let motion imitate the medium — presses stamp, papers slide. No glows, no parallax hero.
  6. Do at least three critique passes with fresh screenshots before you call it done.
